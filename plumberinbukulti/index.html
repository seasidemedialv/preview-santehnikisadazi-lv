In Riga’s Bukulti neighbourhood, construction of a water main and a pressure sewer is planned for 2026–2027, while in the village of Bukulti in Garkalne Parish by Lielais Baltezers many houses have grown out of former garden plots. The main jobs follow from that: new indoor plumbing, pumps and yard lines that do not freeze.

Neighbourhood and village: where the boundary runs

In 1974 Bukulti was included in Riga, but the northern part of the historic centre stayed outside the city. Riga’s Bukulti neighbourhood covers 518.3 ha, while the village of Bukulti in Garkalne Parish, Ropaži Municipality, lies by the Jugla canal and the western shore of Lielais Baltezers and borders Ādaži Parish. On both sides people live in detached houses, but water matters are handled by different companies.

Pressure sewer between the Jugla canal and the railway

In 2024 SIA “Rīgas ūdens” began designing a water main and a pressure sewer between the Jugla canal and the railway. Construction is planned for 2026–2027, and more than 100 households will be able to connect. A pressure sewer was chosen because of the terrain and flood risk: each user pumps wastewater with their own small pumping station, and flood water does not enter the network.

For a house this means different work from an ordinary gravity sewer. All the indoor outlets have to be gathered to one place, from where wastewater flows to the pumping station, and the station needs electricity. When there is no electricity, the pump does not pump, so at such a moment you have to be careful with water use.

Which station and where to put it is decided by the connection design and the water company’s requirements. The plumber’s part is the inside of the house: rearranging the outlets, building the line from the house to the station with the correct fall, and testing the drains before connecting. In indoor drains, 75–100 mm pipes need a fall of 2 cm per metre, and thinner pipes up to 50 mm need 3 cm per metre (LBN 221-15).

In Riga, a private house sewer connection can receive co-funding under regulations No. RD-23-207-sn: up to 5 m in length €1,000, 5–20 m €1,500, over 20 m €2,000. A water supply connection is not co-funded, and the application must be submitted within three years after the work is finished. Checked 13.11.2026.

Land a metre above sea level: how to plan a cellar and a pump

In the low parts around the Jugla canal groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m, and the terrain is about 1 m above sea level. On the shores of Ķīšezers, Jugla and the canal the flood risk over large areas exceeds 5%, in places 10%. On the Riga side, of five detached-house plots only one is built up, Bukulti along Kanāla Street; the rest are family garden plots that can be developed if flooding is dealt with. This place has long been known for groundwater: in 1883 the hydrologist Ā. Tīms found a wide groundwater-rich area for Riga’s water supply within the bounds of the Bukulti, Remberģi and Zaķi manors.

A utility room or cellar below ground level in such a place needs a pump. The inlet strainer of a drainage pump must be fully under water during operation, and the pump is placed on a solid base so that silt does not clog the strainer (manufacturer Grundfos). A permanently installed pump with a float switch needs a non-return valve on the discharge.

If the street sewer is higher than the cellar outlet, wastewater has to be pumped above the backflow level with a pumping station and a loop. For a toilet in the cellar, a small unit such as a Sololift-type pump is suitable; the manufacturer (Grundfos) does, however, require another toilet in the house above the backflow level.

From a garden plot to a year-round house

In the village of Bukulti in Garkalne Parish, from the 1950s to the 1980s land by the canal was allotted to Riga residents for detached houses. In 1986 the central part was assigned to 368 garden plots, and permanent detached houses have been built there since the 1990s. In 2000 the village had 143 residents, and in 2022 already 1,283.

If a garden house has been rebuilt for year-round living, the system meant for summer usually has to be rebuilt. This is work best planned before the walls and floors are finished:

  • lay the external water pipe at least 0.5 m deeper than the depth at which 0 °C is possible in the ground (LBN 222-15, point 164);
  • insulate pipes in unheated rooms with no gaps at bends, valves and joints;
  • pressure-test the new water pipe before covering it and flush it with drinking water; the test pressure and method are stated in the building design;
  • test the indoor drains with air so that every trap keeps its water seal.

If the house stands empty in winter, the incoming supply valve is closed and the system is drained. The pump’s chambers must also be drained, otherwise frozen water can damage the internal parts (manufacturer Grundfos).

Water quality on the village network and the water heater

SIA “Garkalnes inženiertīkli” also publishes water reports for Bukulti: in the sample from 1 June 2026 at 6 Neļķu Street the water pH was 7.6 and the electrical conductivity 652 µS/cm. Hardness is not stated in these reports, so nothing can be concluded from them about limescale in a water heater.

The condition of a water heater is better seen at servicing. The manufacturer (Ariston) advises descaling the heating element about every 2 years, more often in very hard water. Water heater cleaning (descaling) and servicing costs from €75; a new anode or seal is extra.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to a pressure sewer if the electricity goes off?

A pumping station does not pump without electricity, so then you should use as little water as possible. How much wastewater the station can store and whether it has an alarm is stated by its manufacturer and the connection design.

Our house is in the village of Bukulti in Garkalne Parish. Which rules apply to us?

The Ropaži Municipality rules. Water and sewerage are provided by SIA “Garkalnes inženiertīkli”, you can apply for connection co-funding under binding regulations No. 34/23, and a septic tank or holding tank must be registered under regulations No. 45/22; for new builds this is done before the building is accepted for use.

Can a toilet be installed in a cellar if groundwater is this shallow?

It can, if the wastewater is pumped higher. Small units under EN 12050-3 are intended precisely for places where a natural fall cannot be made; if a fall can be made, the manufacturer (Grundfos) advises gravity drainage.

Can the old pipes of a garden house be left as they are?

It depends on where and how deep they lie and whether they are insulated. In a shallow line in the yard or in an unheated room, water freezes in winter, and ice can burst a pipe. A plumber call-out with an inspection costs from €40, and the inspection shows what can stay.
