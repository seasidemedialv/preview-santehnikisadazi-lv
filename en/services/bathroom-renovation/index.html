What a plumber does in a bathroom and what not

A plumber's work in a bathroom is pipes and fixtures. That means cold and hot water supply pipes to every fixture, drain outlets with the correct fall, installing the bath, shower, toilet and sink, connecting mixer taps and sealing the joints between a fixture and the wall.

Tiles, waterproofing, plasterboard walls and closing the chases are another trade. The electrics for a whirlpool bath, a shower cabin with lighting or a heated towel rail are connected by an electrician. If you rebuild a bathroom completely, a plumber is usually needed twice: first for the outlets, before the wall is closed and tiled, and after tiling to install the fixtures.

In houses the bathroom is often on the ground floor next to the utility room with the water heater and hydrophore (a pump with a pressure tank). In older houses and in summer houses rebuilt for year-round living it can also be on the attic floor or in the basement by the sauna. That determines how far the outlet has to be run and whether there is enough room for the fall.

When you need a plumber and when you can manage yourself

You need a plumber if water appears under the bath or shower cabin, if water stays in the tray, if a mixer tap drips even when closed. You also need one if you want a shower in place of the bath, if a new bathroom is needed in the attic or basement, or if the silicone seam is black and peeling and water leaks behind the bath.

Not every case calls for a call-out:

  • A short drip right after the mixer tap is closed is physically normal and not a defect (hansgrohe).
  • If the flow has become weak and uneven, first clean the aerator at the end of the tap: the maker Neoperl states that limescale builds up in it over time.
  • If water drains slowly from a shower tray, Geberit advises pulling the hair out of the trap with long tweezers, because a shower trap cannot easily be cleaned without removing the tray.
  • If a rarely used guest bathroom smells, the water in the trap may have evaporated. Open the tap for a few seconds, and the water seal in the trap is restored.

Bath replacement

A steel or acrylic bath is installed on feet or a frame, and the waste and overflow are connected. A cast iron bath or a bath in a tiled panel is harder. More on the page bath replacement.

Bath to shower conversion

Here the drain outlet decides everything. A bath outlet is low by the floor, but a shower tray or drain needs room for the trap and the fall. The options are described on the page bath to shower conversion.

Bath sealing

The silicone seam between the bath and the wall is renewed when it has turned black, has come away or water leaks behind the bath. New silicone is not put over the old: the old is removed completely. More on the page bath sealing.

Shower cabin installation

A shower cabin is installed in a prepared space: the walls and floor must be ready and the outlets in place. What matters most is a levelled tray and access to the trap. The shower drain option is also described there: shower cabin installation.

Shower cabin repair

Most often a shower cabin leaks through the door seals, the silicone seams or the trap connection under the tray. Where to look for the cause, depending on where the water appears, see the page shower cabin repair.

Shower system installation

A rain shower with a thermostat can be surface-mounted (on the existing outlets) or built into the wall, which is done before tiling. A thermostat needs equal hot and cold water pressure. More: shower system installation.

What to consider in a bathroom in a house

A drain needs a fall. LBN 221-15 states that pipes up to 50 mm are laid with a 30 ‰ fall (3 cm per metre), and 75–100 mm pipes with a 20 ‰ fall (2 cm per metre). For a bath, shower and kitchen sink the minimum outlet diameter is 40 mm, for a toilet 100 mm and for a drain 50 or 100 mm. In an attic bathroom that determines where a shower and toilet can go.

If a bathroom or sauna is in a basement below the cover of the nearest inspection chamber, under LBN 221-15 the fixtures are connected with a separate outlet and a device that prevents the basement from flooding from the external sewer (a backflow valve).

Hot water at the draw-off point must be 55–70 °C. The maker Ariston warns that water hotter than 50 °C can cause serious burns at once, especially to children and older people, and advises fitting a thermostatic mixing valve on the hot water outlet. That is why a thermostatic shower mixer tap suits a home with children.

In a rarely used bathroom or summer house the water in the trap evaporates, and a sewer smell appears. SPKC advises running hot and cold water for a few minutes at least once a week from rarely used taps and showers, also after a longer absence, to limit legionella.

Falls, outlet diameters, the basement connection and hot water temperature: Cabinet Regulation No. 332, LBN 221-15 “Internal water supply and sewerage of buildings” (likumi.lv), checked 13.11.2026.

What most often goes wrong in a bathroom

  • Fixtures are bought before the outlets are checked, and for the new bath or tray the outlet is not in the right place.
  • The walls are tiled before the outlets are tested with water, and a leak can later be found only by opening the tiles.
  • New silicone is applied over the old, it does not hold, and it leaks behind the bath again.
  • The trap under the tray has no access, and a blockage cannot be cleared without taking the cabin apart.
  • A thermostat is installed in a house where the hot and cold water pressure differ.

Frequently asked questions

Do you do tiling and waterproofing?

No. We do bathroom plumbing: pipes, outlets and fixtures. Tiling and waterproofing are done by another tradesperson, and the plumbing work must be finished and tested before they start.

Can I buy the fixtures myself?

Yes, and that is often done. Before buying, compare the outlet position given in the fixture's instructions with where the outlets are in your bathroom, and write in the form what you have bought.

How much does a full replacement of bathroom plumbing cost?

The work costs from €450 in a standard bathroom. Materials, tiling and closing the chases are extra. The price depends on moving outlets, removing an old cast iron bath and how many fixtures there are.
