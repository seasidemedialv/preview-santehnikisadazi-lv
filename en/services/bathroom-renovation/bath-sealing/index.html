Bath sealing in Ādaži - renewing the bath seams

Bath sealing in Ādaži and Riga Region means removing the old silicone seam and making a new one between the bath and the wall, so that water does not run behind the bath.

The same is done at a shower cabin, tray and sink. The work is charged at an hourly rate from €25/h, materials extra. If water appears under the bath even when the seam is intact, the cause may be the drain, not the seam.

When a bath seam needs renewing

  • The seam has black mould spots that do not wash off, because the mould is inside the silicone.
  • The silicone has come away from the tile or the bath edge, and a gap is visible between them.
  • After a shower, water stays behind or under the bath, and damp patches appear on the wall below the bath level.
  • The seam has cracked, because an acrylic bath settles slightly when filled with water.

The work on this page is the seam. If the whole bath must be replaced, see bath replacement.

Seam or leak: how to tell the difference

Not all water under a bath comes through the seam. Fill the bath and let the water out without wetting the walls. If water appears under the bath, the drain or overflow connection is leaking, not the seam. If water appears only after a shower, when splashes run down the wall, the fault is most likely in the seam.

If there is damp on the wall or floor even when the bath is not used, a water supply pipe in the wall may be leaking. That is no longer a sealing job: see water leak.

How bath sealing works

  1. Removing the old silicone The old seam is cut out and removed completely, including the thin film on the tile and the bath edge. New silicone does not hold well on old.
  2. Cleaning and drying The surfaces are cleaned and degreased. The seam area must be dry, otherwise the silicone will come away.
  3. Bath with water The bath is often filled with water before sealing, so that it settles as it does in use and the seam does not split later.
  4. The new seam Sanitary silicone is applied, smoothed in one pass and the masking tape is removed.

How long silicone takes to cure

It depends on the specific silicone. For example, for its sanitary silicone Sikasil-117 Sanitary the maker Sika states that a skin forms in about 15 minutes, and the seam cures to about 3 mm depth in 24 hours at 23 °C and 50% humidity. A thicker seam cures longer. How long to keep off the bath is on the packaging of your silicone.

For this silicone the maker states application at an air temperature from +5 to +45 °C. In an unheated summer house in winter or early spring, the bathroom must be warmed before the work.

Sealing a shower cabin, tray and sink

The seams are renewed in the same way between the shower tray and the wall, between the cabin profiles and the tray, between the sink and the wall and around the base of the toilet. For a shower cabin you first have to work out whether the seam or the door seals are leaking: about that on the page shower cabin repair.

How much does bath sealing cost?

Sealing is charged at an hourly rate: from €25/h, and the minimum charge is usually 1 hour. If the cause of the leak has to be found, a plumber's call-out with inspection costs from €40 and, if the work is done on the spot, the call-out is usually included in the labour price. If it turns out that the drain is leaking, fixing the leak costs from €40.

The price depends on the length of the seams, how hard the old silicone is to remove and whether the shower and sink are sealed at the same visit. This is the labour price; materials are extra. All prices: plumbing prices.

Frequently asked questions

Can new silicone be applied over the old?

No. New silicone does not hold well on old, and the mould under it stays. The old seam is removed completely.

Can mould in a seam be cleaned off?

Surface dirt can. If the black spots are inside the silicone, they will not clean off, and the seam is replaced.

How long after the work can the bath not be used?

It depends on the silicone and the thickness of the seam. Sikasil-117 Sanitary cures to about 3 mm depth in 24 hours at 23 °C, and a thicker seam takes longer. See the instructions of your silicone's maker.

Can I renew the seam myself?

You can, if the old silicone is removed completely and the surface is dry and degreased. It is worth calling a plumber if water leaks under the bath even with an intact seam or if the wall behind the bath is already damp, because then a leak has to be found.

Why does the seam at an acrylic bath split?

An acrylic bath settles slightly when filled with water. If the seam is made with an empty bath, it can crack. That is why the bath is often filled before sealing.
