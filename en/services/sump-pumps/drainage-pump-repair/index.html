Drainage pump repair in Ādaži

Drainage pump repair in Ādaži and the Riga region usually starts not with the motor but with what is around it: a stuck float switch, a strainer clogged with silt, a faulty non-return valve or a frozen outlet.

If the pump does not switch on in spring, when the groundwater rises, you need to act quickly. A call-out with inspection costs from €40, labour from €25/h.

The drainage pump will not switch on

  • The float is stuck against the wall of the pit or the pump body and does not rise with the water.
  • There is no power: the circuit breaker or the residual-current device has tripped. If it trips again, do not switch the pump on once more and call an electrician.
  • The pump hums but the water does not move: the strainer or inlet is blocked, or the impeller is jammed.
  • In winter or just after it: the outlet outside is frozen and the water has nowhere to go.

The maker of the Grundfos Unilift KP requires the inlet strainer to be fully under water during operation and the pump to stand on a solid base, otherwise silt blocks the strainer. Often it is enough to lift the pump out and clean the strainer and the bottom of the pit.

The drainage pump will not switch off or starts too often

If the pump runs without stopping, the float may be stuck in the upper position, or the water may be coming in faster than the pump can remove it. If it switches on every few seconds, the cause is often the non-return valve: after each stop the water from the outlet pipe flows back into the pit. For a permanently installed pump with a float switch, the maker Grundfos makes a non-return valve on the discharge mandatory.

The second cause is a float cable that is too short. The float switch turns the pump on and off by level, and a longer free cable gives fewer starts and a bigger difference between the levels. For the Unilift KP pump the maker gives a free cable length of 63.5–152 mm.

Float switch replacement

If the float is cracked, water has got into it or the contact no longer works, it is replaced. A separate float switch can be replaced on pumps where it is external; if the float is built into the body, it is often more sensible to replace the whole pump. On the call you talk through this once the pump model is known.

How the repair works

  1. Power off The pump is disconnected from the mains before it is lifted out of the pit.
  2. Inspection The float movement, strainer, impeller, cable and the outlet line up to the discharge point are checked.
  3. Non-return valve and outlet We check that the non-return valve closes and that the outlet is not blocked or frozen.
  4. The pit The silt is cleared out, and we make sure the pump stands on a solid base and the float has free space.
  5. Test Water is poured into the pit to see whether the pump switches on and off at the right level.

Drainage pump replacement

The pump is replaced if the motor or body is damaged, if spare parts are not available, or if the repair would cost almost as much as a new pump. A pump that is not in use in winter must be protected from frost: Grundfos states that during a frost period an unused pump must have all the water drained from its pump chambers, otherwise frozen water can damage the internal parts. Installing a new pump is described on the page sump pumps.

When it is worth checking the pump

A basement pump can sit idle for months and then fail exactly when it is needed. LVĢMC (the Latvian Environment, Geology and Meteorology Centre) data show that groundwater in Latvia is usually highest in the second half of March and in April, and again in October and November. So it is worth checking the pump before these months: pour water into the pit and watch whether the pump switches on, pumps out and switches off.

How much does drainage pump repair cost?

A plumber's call-out with inspection and diagnosis costs from €40, and if the work is done right away, the call-out is usually included in the price of the work. Frequently asked questions

What should I do if the pump is not working and the water is rising?

Switch off the power to the pump if that is not dangerous, and check whether the float moves freely. If the water already reaches electrical appliances, do not step into it. What to do first is described on the page about water in the basement.

Why does the pump hum but not pump?

Often the strainer or inlet is blocked, the impeller is jammed or the outlet is frozen. If the strainer is clean and the outlet is clear, the motor may be faulty.

Is it enough to replace only the float?

If the float is external and the rest of the pump works, yes. If it is built into the body, it may be more sensible to replace the whole pump.
