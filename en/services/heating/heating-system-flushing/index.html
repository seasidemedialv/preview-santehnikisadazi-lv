Heating system flushing in a house in Ādaži

Heating system flushing in Ādaži and the Riga region is needed when radiators stay cold at the bottom, the heating water is dark, or new radiators or underfloor loops are connected to the system.

Flushing removes sludge and iron oxide, then the system is refilled, bled and, if needed, protected with an inhibitor. The price depends on the system volume and the method, and on the call you talk through what your house needs.

When flushing is needed

The maker Worcester Bosch states that if a radiator is warm at the top but cold at the bottom, the cause is usually sludge, and then the system needs cleaning, not bleeding. Other signs are dark, black water from the bleed valve, a pump that often seizes, and radiators that heat up less and less.

The maker Vaillant advises testing the heating water with a magnetic rod. If it picks up magnetite (iron oxide), the system should be flushed and protected against corrosion with an inhibitor, or a magnetic filter should be fitted.

Flushing is also needed when the system is new or has been altered. Vaillant states that the whole heating system must be flushed before the heat source is started, and that an inhibitor, if used, is added after flushing according to the inhibitor maker's instructions. If new radiators or water underfloor heating installation are added to an old system, the old deposits end up in the new parts without flushing.

How flushing a house differs from a block of flats

In a block of flats the shared building system is usually flushed on the order of the property manager, not flat by flat. In a house the system is small and easy to oversee: you can flush each radiator separately, see what comes out of it, and replace a rusted valve or a seized pump straight away.

In older houses with steel pipes and cast-iron radiators there is usually more sludge. In houses with underfloor heating, deposits can also block the flow meters on the manifold.

How a heating system is cleaned

  1. Water check A sample of heating water is taken from the bleed valve or the drain valve, to see how dark it is and whether it contains magnetite.
  2. Choosing the method Hydraulic flushing pushes water through the radiators one by one at a high flow rate. Chemical flushing means a cleaning agent circulates in the system for a while, loosens the deposits, and is then flushed out.
  3. Flushing in sections Radiators and loops are flushed one at a time until clean water comes out. If the boiler heat exchanger also needs cleaning, that is a separate job.
  4. Refilling The system is filled with fresh water, bled, and the pressure is set.
  5. Inhibitor and filter After flushing, an inhibitor is added according to its maker's instructions. If there was a lot of magnetite, Vaillant mentions a magnetic filter as an option.

Filling and balancing the heating system

After flushing, the system is filled to the correct pressure. In a closed system, cold, this is usually 1–2 bar: the maker Vaillant states 1.0–2.0 bar, Worcester Bosch advises 1–1.5 bar. Flamco and Vaillant state that the system should be filled so that the pressure is 0.2 bar higher than the pre-charge of the expansion vessel.

Balancing means adjusting the radiator valves so that the water does not take the shortest route and flow only to the nearest radiators. If after flushing the near radiators are hot but the farther ones are lukewarm, the system needs balancing.

How often to check the heating water

The maker Fernox states that under the British standard BS 7593:2019 the heating system water should be tested every year. This does not mean the system must be flushed every year: the test shows whether the water is clean and the inhibitor is still working.

How much heating system flushing costs

Flushing has no single starting price, because the job differs a lot from house to house. The price depends on:

  • the system volume and the number of radiators or circuits;
  • whether the flushing is chemical or hydraulic;
  • whether the boiler heat exchanger needs cleaning;
  • the price of the inhibitor and the new heat-transfer fluid.

Frequently asked questions

Does flushing help if a radiator is cold at the top?

No, then the radiator usually has air in it, and bleeding is enough. Flushing helps when the bottom is cold. We wrote about both cases on the page radiator repair.

Can only one radiator be flushed?

It can: the radiator is shut off, removed and flushed separately. But if there is sludge throughout the system, it will build up again after a while, so the whole system is usually flushed.

Should the system be flushed before new radiators are fitted?

If the old system is dirty, yes. Otherwise sludge from the old pipes ends up in the new radiators. More on the page radiator replacement.

Can a system with antifreeze be flushed?

It can. The old fluid has to be collected, and after flushing the system is filled with new heat-transfer fluid intended for a closed heating loop.
