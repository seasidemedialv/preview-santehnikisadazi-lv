In a house you do not have to ask a manager for permission, and the system can be shut off and refilled on the spot. Describe in the form how many radiators need replacing and where they are.

What is different about replacing a radiator in a house

In a flat, a radiator is part of the whole building's system, and shutting off the riser has to be arranged through the manager. In a house the system is yours, so the replacement can be planned when it suits you, and several radiators can be replaced at once or a new one added.

On the other hand, in a house there is more to find out about the system itself. Three things matter:

  • whether the system is single-pipe or two-pipe: on a single-pipe loop a radiator usually has a bypass, and without it, replacing a radiator can leave the next ones cold as well;
  • how the radiator is connected: from the side or from the bottom, and whether the connections of the new radiator match the existing pipes;
  • whether the radiator has shut-off valves: if it does, closing them is enough for the replacement; if not, part of the system is drained.

How radiators are replaced

  1. Shutting off the system The radiator valves are closed, or the part of the system the radiator is in is drained. The water is let out through the drain valve.
  2. Removing the old radiator The supply pipes are disconnected and the radiator is lifted off its brackets. An old steel or cast-iron radiator often holds sludge, so it is removed carefully to keep the dirty water off the floor.
  3. Fitting the new radiator The brackets are fixed, the radiator is hung level and connected with valves. If the supply pipes do not match, short pipe sections are reworked.
  4. Filling and bleeding The system is filled, the radiator is bled until the hissing stops and water starts to flow steadily, and the joints are checked.
  5. Pressure check After bleeding the pressure usually drops, so the pressure gauge is checked and, if needed, the system is topped up.

If the old radiators are warm only at the top, there is usually sludge in the system. Then, before connecting the new radiators, it is worth considering heating system flushing, otherwise the deposits will reach the new radiators too.

Installing new radiators in a house

If a radiator is added to a room that did not have one (for example an extension or an insulated attic), the job is bigger than a replacement: a new supply and a return line have to be run from the nearest heating pipe. On a single-pipe system you need to make sure the new radiator does not worsen the heat of the radiators after it.

Before buying, find out the connection type and the distance between the connections. If you have already bought the radiator, note it in the form.

Moving radiators and replacing heating pipes

A radiator can be moved to another wall or under another window if pipes can be run to the new spot: along the floor, in the wall or above the skirting board. The longer the section and the more walls it has to cross, the more expensive the job.

In older houses the pipes leading to radiators are often steel pipes rusted right at the valves. When changing a radiator it makes sense to replace this section too, rather than connect a new radiator to an old pipe that will soon start to leak.

How much radiator replacement costs

Installing or replacing a radiator costs from €75 each. This includes removing the old radiator, fixing the new one and connecting it to the existing supply pipes, filling and bleeding. If pipes have to be replaced or extended, pipe replacement or installation costs from €10/m. This is the labour price; materials are extra.

The price is affected by the size and weight of the radiator, the connection type, whether the radiator has valves, and how much pipework has to be redone. In a block of flats, shutting off the riser (arranged with the building manager) is not included.

Frequently asked questions

Can a radiator be replaced during the heating season?

Yes. If the radiator has shut-off valves, the heating in the rest of the house may not even have to be switched off. If there are no valves, part of the system is drained, and then the heating does not work for a while.

Should the valves be replaced together with the radiator?

Usually yes, if the old valves are rusted or do not close. With new thermostatic valves the temperature of each room can be adjusted separately.

Which radiator should I buy?

One whose connection type and distance between connections suit your pipes, and whose size suits the room. On the call you talk through what to consider in your house and who buys the radiator.

What if a radiator simply does not heat?

Then it often does not need replacing. First read about radiator repair: air, sludge or a closed valve are the most common causes.
