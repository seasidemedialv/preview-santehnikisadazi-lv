The most important thing is to know what the rail is connected to: the heating loop or hot water circulation. Write in the form what kind of rail you have now and whether the new one has already been bought.

What a heated towel rail is connected to in a house

In a block of flats a water heated towel rail is usually on the hot water riser, and replacing it means shutting off the riser through the manager. In a house the way the rail is connected decides how it will work:

  • on the heating loop: the rail works like a small radiator, is warm in the heating season and cold in summer;
  • on hot water circulation: the rail is warm all year, but only if the house has a hot water circulation line and pump;
  • if neither is available, or the rail needs heat in summer too, a combined rail with an electric heating element is chosen, and an electrician makes its electrical connection.

If the old rail was cold in summer, a new one in exactly the same place will be cold too. That is not a fault but the way it is connected.

How a heated towel rail is installed

  1. Shutting off The valves of the rail, or of the part of the system it is connected to, are closed. If there are no valves, the heating loop or hot water part is drained.
  2. Removing the old one The supply pipes are disconnected and the old rail is taken off. In older houses the supply pipes are often steel pipes rusted at the joint, and then this section is redone as well.
  3. Fitting the new one The brackets are fixed, the distance between the connections is checked against the supply pipes, and the rail is connected with valves on both sides so that next time it can be shut off on its own.
  4. Filling and bleeding The system is filled and the rail is bled through the top valve. If it is on a heating loop, the pressure is checked after bleeding, because it usually drops.

Connecting a heated towel rail in a new place

If the rail has to move to another wall, or the bathroom did not have one before, new supply pipes are needed. They are usually run from the nearest heating or hot water pipe in the wall or floor, which means chasing and making good the finish, done separately. It is best done during bathroom renovation, before tiling.

If more than the rail changes in the bathroom, see the page bathroom plumbing.

What to find out before buying a new rail

  • the distance between the axes of the old rail's supply pipes, and whether the pipes come from the wall or the floor;
  • whether the new rail is meant for the connection your house has: a heating loop, hot water circulation or both;
  • whether the supply pipes have valves that let the rail be shut off without draining the whole system;
  • the size and shape: whether the rail will fit between the tiles, the door and the bath or shower.

If you write these things in the form, on the call we can say right away whether replacing it on the existing supply pipes is enough or whether those need redoing too.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my heated towel rail cold in summer?

Most likely it is connected to the heating loop, and the heating does not run in summer. For it to be warm all year it needs a connection to hot water circulation, or a combined rail with an electric heating element.

Will the new rail fit the old supply pipes?

If the distance between the connections and the connection type match, yes. If not, the supply pipes are reworked a little. Before buying, measure the distance between the old rail's supply pipes.

Do you also connect an electric heated towel rail?

The water side, yes. The electrical connection for a combined or electric rail is made by an electrician.
