How a heating system works in a house

Most houses have their own heat source and a closed water heating system. The heat source heats the water, the circulation pump drives it through the pipes to the radiators or floor loops, and the expansion vessel takes up the extra volume when the water warms.

Older houses and summer houses converted for year-round living often have a mixed system: some steel pipes from earlier times, some new plastic pipes, sometimes an open expansion tank in the attic. In such a house, the first step before any repair is to find out what system there is and where it starts and ends.

We do not repair or install the heat source itself, for example a gas boiler or a heat pump. Our work is the water side that follows it. Electric underfloor heating is installed by an electrician, so here we talk only about water underfloor heating.

When you need a plumber and when you can manage yourself

Not every cold radiator needs a call-out. If a radiator is colder at the top than at the bottom, it usually has air in it. You can bleed it yourself: switch the heating off, let the radiators cool, open the bleed valve with a key until the hissing stops and water starts to flow steadily, then close it. After bleeding the pressure usually drops, so look at the pressure gauge and, if needed, top up the system. More about the checks is on the page radiator not heating.

You need a plumber if:

  • a radiator, valve or joint leaks;
  • the pressure keeps dropping, even after topping up;
  • a radiator is cold at the bottom but warm at the top (this is usually a sign of sludge);
  • the circulation pump hums, knocks or does not run, and the radiators farthest away stay cold;
  • a room with underfloor heating does not heat;
  • a radiator or heated towel rail has to be replaced, moved or added.

Replacing a heating circulation pump

If the circulation pump stops, the heat source works but the radiators farthest away stay cold. Signs of a fault are a hum without flow, knocking, a pump that no longer starts after standing idle over the summer, or moisture around the housing.

Not every noise means a fault. For the ALPHA2 pump the maker Grundfos states that it bleeds itself and noise caused by air goes away after a few minutes of running; for faster bleeding, speed III is set briefly. The same maker warns that the pump must not run dry and that the system cannot be bled through the pump.

When replacing a pump, the valves on both sides of it are closed. If there are no valves, part of the system is drained and valves are fitted at the same time, so that it does not have to be done next time. Then the system is filled, bled and the pressure is checked.

Expansion vessel: checking and replacing

An expansion vessel has a diaphragm with gas on one side and heating water on the other. The pre-charge is the gas pressure in the vessel before the system is filled. The makers Flamco and Vaillant state that the system should be filled so that the pressure is 0.2 bar higher than the vessel pre-charge.

Flamco advises checking the vessel and its pre-charge every year. The pre-charge is measured and adjusted when the system has cooled and is without pressure, and the water side of the vessel must be empty. If water comes out of the vessel's air valve, the diaphragm is damaged, and Vaillant states that the vessel then has to be replaced.

A typical sign that the vessel no longer works: when the system warms up, the pressure rises sharply, when it cools it drops, and the safety valve starts to drip.

Pressure dropping in the heating system

In a closed heating system the pressure when cold should usually be 1–2 bar. The maker Vaillant states 1.0–2.0 bar, Worcester Bosch advises filling to 1–1.5 bar. If the system spans several floors, a higher filling pressure is usually needed: Vaillant states that every 10 m of height above the boiler adds about 1 bar.

If the pressure drops once after bleeding, that is normal. If it drops regularly, the cause is usually one of these:

  • a leak: at a radiator valve, a joint or a pipe running in the floor or wall;
  • an expansion vessel with no pre-charge or a damaged diaphragm;
  • a safety valve that releases water because the pressure rises too high as the system warms;
  • repeated bleeding, because air keeps getting into the system.

Topping up the system every week is not a solution. Each top-up brings fresh water into the system, but the cause remains. So the first step is to find where the water is going and to check the vessel.

Installing a heating system in a house

In a new build or when rebuilding an old house, the whole water heating side can be installed: pipe routes from the heat source room, radiators, underfloor heating loops with a manifold, and a heated towel rail in the bathroom. The heat source is installed by its supplier or a specialist, and the system is connected to its outlets.

The maker Vaillant states that the whole heating system must be flushed before the heat source is started, and that an inhibitor, if used, is added after flushing according to the inhibitor maker's instructions. Laying pipes costs from €10/m, the rest at the hourly rate from €25/h. For house plumbing as a whole, see the page plumbing in a new build.

What to write in the request form

Photos cannot be sent in the form, so describe the situation in text. These help:

  • what is happening: leaking, not heating, pressure dropping, the pump is noisy, and since when;
  • the type of home: a one-storey or two-storey house, summer house, terraced house or flat;
  • the heat source, and whether the system is closed or has an open tank in the attic, if you know;
  • radiators, underfloor heating or both, and how many;
  • what the pressure gauge shows with the system cold and warm;
  • the water supply: central, or your own well or borehole, because the system is topped up from it.

What most often goes wrong in a heating system

  • A radiator is bled again and again although it is cold at the bottom. Then the cause is usually sludge, and the maker Worcester Bosch advises cleaning the system, not bleeding.
  • New radiators or underfloor loops are connected to an old, dirty system without flushing, and deposits reach the new parts.
  • The pump is switched on in an empty system, although it must not run dry.
  • In an unheated summer house, water is left in the heating loops for winter. How to prepare the system for winter is on the page plumbing winterising.
  • Antifreeze is poured into a system that is connected to drinking water. For example, UK water regulations classify ethylene glycol fluid as a slight health hazard, so it is used only in a closed heating loop.

Frequently asked questions

Do you also repair a gas boiler or a heat pump?

No. We work on the water heating side: pipes, radiators, valves, the circulation pump, the expansion vessel, underfloor heating loops and the heated towel rail. The heat source itself is serviced and repaired by its service specialist.

What should the pressure in the heating system be?

In a closed system, cold, usually 1–2 bar. The maker Vaillant states 1.0–2.0 bar, Worcester Bosch advises 1–1.5 bar. A two-storey house may need a little more, because height raises the pressure. The exact value is set by the instructions of your heat source and expansion vessel.

Do you also work in flats?

Yes, but in a block of flats the manager of the building arranges shutting off the riser and filling the system, and that is separate. In a house there is no such arrangement.
