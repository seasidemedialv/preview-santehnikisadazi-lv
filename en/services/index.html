Plumber for a house: what is usually needed

In a house, a plumber's range of work is wider than in a flat. The drains inside the building run from the fixtures to the first inspection chamber outside the building, so the house owner is also responsible for the outlet through the foundation and the line in the yard. In a house with its own well or borehole, a hydrophore (a pump with a pressure tank) and a pressure tank come on top, and the basement often has a drainage or sewage pump.

New builds, reconstruction, regular maintenance and a check before selling a house have a separate page: plumbing for a house. For a small job that is hard to place in any group, see master plumber.

Where to start if you do not know what is wrong

  • Water is leaking or a pipe has burst: emergency plumber and pipe repair.
  • Water drains slowly or the drains smell: drain cleaning.
  • Weak pressure or the pump switches on often: hydrophores.
  • No hot water or the water heater leaks: water heaters.
  • Water in the basement or a pump does not work: drainage and sewage pumps.
  • A radiator does not heat or the heating pressure drops: heating.
  • A pipe has frozen or the house must be prepared for winter: plumbing winterising.
  • A toilet, sink or washing machine needs installing: fixture installation.
  • Bath, shower or shower cabin: bathroom plumbing; taps: mixer taps.

What we do not do

Pumping out and installing septic tanks, drilling boreholes, gas installations, water meters, tiling and electric underfloor heating are other specialists' work. At a septic tank, a plumber works only on the house side: the drains inside the house, the outlet from the house and the line to the septic tank.
