If water collects at the floor or the toilet does not flush away, the fault is elsewhere, and so is the job. In the form, write what you see and hear, and the signs already show what needs fixing.

The sign shows where to look for the fault

What you see Where the fault usually is What has to be done
After flushing, water keeps running into the bowl dirt or limescale under the flush valve seal, a worn seal cleaning the valve or replacing the flush mechanism
The cistern fills slowly, does not fill or hisses the fill valve replacing the fill valve
The cistern overflows into the overflow pipe the fill valve does not close at the right level adjusting or replacing the fill valve
A puddle at the base of the toilet the toilet's connection to the drain, or the water supply resealing the joint, sometimes removing the toilet
Water rises and drains away slowly a blockage in the toilet or the drain pipe clearing the blockage with a drain snake
The toilet moves loose fixings refixing it and checking the joint

The maker Geberit writes that continuous running after flushing usually means that dirt or limescale has got between the flush valve seal and the outlet opening.

What you can check yourself

  • If water runs into the bowl: Geberit advises taking out the flush valve, cleaning it with water or a dry cloth and putting it back. If that does not help, the seal or the mechanism is worn.
  • If there is a puddle: wipe the floor and the outside of the toilet dry and watch where the first drop appears. At the cistern supply it is a different fault than at the base of the toilet.
  • If you need to stop the water: there is usually a small shut-off valve under the cistern or next to it. If it does not close fully, write that in the form, because then the main stopcock will have to be shut.

If the toilet overflows after flushing, do not flush again. It is a sign of a blockage, and each next flush puts more water on the floor.

How a cistern repair is done

  1. Emptying the cistern The valve is closed, the water is flushed out and the lid or button is taken off.
  2. Finding the fault It is checked whether water comes through the flush valve or the fill valve does not close, and the seals are inspected.
  3. Replacing the part The fill valve, the flush mechanism or both are replaced. The parts are chosen by the cistern model, because a universal mechanism does not fit every cistern.
  4. Adjusting The cistern is refilled, the water level is set and it is flushed a few times, watching whether the water stops after flushing.

This description applies to a cistern that is visible on the toilet or on the wall. If the cistern is hidden in the wall and only a button is visible, the work is done through the button opening, and it is described on the page wall-hung toilet repair.

A toilet leaking at the floor

Water at the base can come from three places: from the cistern supply, from the joint between the cistern and the toilet, or from the toilet outlet connection to the drain. The first two can usually be fixed without removing the toilet. If the outlet collar leaks, the toilet often has to be removed, the collar replaced and the toilet put back.

If the floor around the toilet is soft or the tiles move, water has been leaking there for a long time. After the repair it is worth checking the floor as well, but that is no longer plumbing work.

When a repair is no longer worth it

A crack in porcelain cannot be repaired: if the toilet or the cistern is cracked, it is replaced. The same goes for very old cisterns whose parts are no longer made and a new mechanism does not fit. Then it is cheaper to replace the whole set, and that is described on the page toilet installation.

How much does toilet repair cost?

  • Cistern repair (mechanism replacement): from €35, parts are extra.
  • Clearing a toilet blockage: from €50. If the toilet has to be removed, it costs more.
  • A leak at a joint or the supply: fixing the leak from €40.
  • If the toilet has to be replaced: from €50.

A plumber's call-out costs from €40; if the repair is done straight away, it is usually included in the labour price.

Frequently asked questions

Does a running cistern waste a lot of water?

Yes, if it runs all the time, the water flows at night too. With your own well that means the hydrophore (a pump with a pressure tank) switches on more often, and with the central water supply a bigger bill.

Do you replace just the button?

Yes, if the fault is in the button or its mechanism. In the form, write the maker of the cistern if it is visible, so that the part can be sourced.

Can a cracked toilet be repaired?

No. A crack in porcelain cannot be repaired, so a cracked toilet or cistern is replaced.

What should I do until the plumber arrives?

Close the shut-off valve at the cistern. If it does not close or water runs onto the floor, close the main stopcock or switch off the hydrophore.
