The trap is replaced if it leaks, is cracked, does not fit a new fixture or the seals no longer hold. If a smell comes from the drain, first check whether there is any water in the trap at all.

What a trap does

A trap is a bend under a fixture in which water stands. This water seal stops sewer air from entering the room. For example, UK guidance (Approved Document H) states that at least 25 mm of water must remain in the trap during operation; for a sink trap it recommends a 75 mm deep seal, and for a bath, shower and toilet 50 mm.

If a fixture is not used for a long time, the water in the trap evaporates and a smell appears. This happens in a guest bathroom, in a summer house after winter or at a floor drain that nobody washes. In such cases the trap does not need replacing: it is enough to pour in water. If the smell returns although the fixture is used, the cause may be different, and it is described in the article sewer smell in the house.

Replacement or cleaning

Sign What usually helps
A smell at a fixture that is rarely used pour in water, the trap does not need replacing
A drip at the nut joint replacing the seal or tightening the nut, if the body is intact
A crack in the plastic body replacing the trap
Water drains slowly cleaning the trap; if that does not help, the blockage is further along the pipe
A new sink or bath with a different waste outlet a new trap that fits the waste outlet and the drain outlet
A washing machine or dishwasher has to be connected a trap with a branch

The trap must be removable or have a cleaning opening, so that a blockage can be cleared, and it is fitted directly after the fixture. LBN 221-15 states that for a fixture without a built-in trap it is installed under the fixture, and fixtures from different rooms must not be connected to one trap.

Replacing a bath and shower trap

A bath trap is harder to reach than a sink trap. It sits under the bath and has two inlets: for the waste outlet and for the overflow. To get to it, the bath panel must be removed. If the panel is tiled in and has no hatch, the job starts with finding access, and that must be discussed before the work.

A shower tray trap cannot easily be cleaned without removing the tray, so the maker Geberit advises pulling hair out with long tweezers. Shower drains to EN 1253 usually have a 50 mm water seal. If the shower trap under the tray leaks, it is often noticed late, when water has already appeared under the floor or on the wall of the next room.

How a trap is replaced

  1. Preparation A container is put under the trap, because water stands in it. For a bath, the panel is removed.
  2. Removing the old trap The trap is unscrewed from the waste outlet and from the drain pipe, and the end of the pipe and the sealing ring in it are inspected.
  3. Fitting the new trap The trap is chosen by the size of the waste outlet and the diameter of the drain outlet, assembled with new seals and tightened by hand, without great force.
  4. Test The fixture is filled and emptied at full flow, watching whether any joint drips.

Frequently asked questions

A sink smells, but the trap is intact. Does it have to be replaced?

Not always. If the sink is rarely used, the water in the trap evaporates; pour in water. If the smell stays, the cause can be a seal, the joint with the pipe or the ventilation of the drains.

Can a sink and a washing machine be connected to one trap?

Yes, if they are in the same room and the trap has a branch. LBN 221-15 does not allow fixtures from different rooms to be connected to one trap.

In a summer house after winter all the drains smell. What should I do?

Most likely the water has evaporated from the traps. Run water in every sink, shower and drain. If the system was drained for winter, it also needs flushing, starting with cold water.

Can just the seal be replaced?

It can, if the trap body is intact. A cracked trap cannot be fixed with a seal.
