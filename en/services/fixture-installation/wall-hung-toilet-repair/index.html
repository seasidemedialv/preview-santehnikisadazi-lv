Why a concealed cistern does not need the wall broken open

In a concealed frame the cistern is inside the wall, and the only access to it is the button opening. Frame makers plan for this: Geberit states that the flush and fill valves of a concealed cistern are changed through the service opening behind the push panel, without opening the wall. The Geberit Sigma cistern has a built-in angle shut-off valve on the supply, so the water can be shut off right there.

Spare parts are also easier to get than many people think. Geberit has extended the availability of spare parts for its concealed cisterns, such as flush and fill valves, from 25 to 50 years. This differs between makers, so the model matters.

Common faults in a concealed cistern

  • Water keeps running into the toilet after flushing: dirt or limescale under the flush valve seal, or a worn seal.
  • The cistern fills slowly or not at all: the fill valve, or a shut-off valve that is not fully open.
  • The button does not return or has to be pressed twice: the panel mechanism or the rods between the button and the valve.
  • The toilet rocks: loose hanging fixings, which over time can also loosen the outlet connection.
  • Water on the wall or under the toilet: a leak on the supply or at a joint inside the frame, which is the more serious fault.

What not to do before the plumber comes

Do not descale the inside of the cistern with products. Geberit warns that cleaning or descaling the inside of a concealed cistern is usually not needed, because loosened pieces of limescale can block the system. Also do not open the tiles before it has been checked whether the fault can be reached through the button opening.

How wall-hung toilet repair is done

  1. Removing the panel The push panel is taken off the frame, and the service opening behind it is exposed.
  2. Shutting off the water The shut-off valve on the cistern supply is closed, or the main stopcock if there is no valve there or it does not hold.
  3. Taking out the valve The flush or fill valve is taken out through the opening, the seals are inspected, and it is decided whether cleaning is enough or the part must be replaced.
  4. Reassembly and test The valve is put back or replaced, the water is turned on and both flush buttons are tested before the panel goes back.

Geberit and other frame repairs

The maker can usually be read on the panel, on the button or on the cistern behind the panel. Write it in the form, together with when the frame was installed, if you know. The right valves can be found from the maker and cistern type, because parts from different makers do not fit each other.

If the frame maker is not known and the parts carry no markings, the plumber identifies it on site, and the part may have to be sourced after the visit.

When the wall does have to be opened

Through the button opening you can reach only the cistern valves. If the supply or the drain connection leaks inside the frame, the cladding has to be opened, and the wall restored after the repair. Tiling is a different trade. If the frame is damaged or does not suit the new toilet, it is replaced, and installing a frame with a wall-hung toilet costs from €85.

Frequently asked questions

Can a concealed cistern be repaired without opening the tiles?

Usually yes. The cistern valves are changed through the opening behind the push panel. The wall is opened only if the supply or a joint inside the frame leaks.

How do I know which frame I have?

The maker is usually on the panel, on the button or on the cistern behind the panel. If you cannot see it, describe the shape of the panel and the number of buttons, and the plumber will confirm on site.

Can Geberit parts still be found for an old frame?

Geberit states that spare parts for concealed cisterns are available for 50 years. For other makers, check by model.

Can the water be shut off for just the toilet?

Many concealed cisterns have a shut-off valve on the supply behind the panel, for example the Geberit Sigma. If there is none, the water is shut off at the main stopcock.
