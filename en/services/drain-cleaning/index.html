Drains in a house: from the sink to the first chamber

A building's internal drains run from the sanitary fixtures to the first inspection chamber outside the building. In a flat a blockage is usually between the fixture and the riser, but in a house the path is longer. It includes the trap, the outlet under the floor, the horizontal line, the house outlet through the foundations and then the line in the yard up to a chamber, a septic tank or the connection to the central network.

So in a house it is important to understand which section the fault is in. If cleaning the trap is not enough, the snake is fed in from the nearest inspection point or from a chamber against the flow. Outside the house the line is usually your responsibility: for example, under Riga regulations the central sewer ends at the outer wall of the inspection chamber, and beyond that the customer maintains the networks on the property.

When you need a plumber and when you can manage yourself

  • Only one sink drains slowly: often it is enough to take the trap apart and clean it. If that does not help, the blockage is deeper in the outlet.
  • Water stands in several fixtures at once or appears at the lowest point (a basement shower, a ground-floor drain): the fault is in the shared horizontal line or the yard line, and that is a job for a snake.
  • The toilet gurgles when the bath or washing machine drains: the water is looking for a way out through another trap, because the line is partly blocked or ventilation is missing.
  • The water stands but the septic tank is full: then the line is not blocked, and you first need a tanker operator. The owner of a septic tank regularly hands the wastewater and sediment to a tanker operator.

A blocked drain in a house: where to look for the fault

Look into the first chamber in the yard. If the chamber is full and the water stands above the pipe, the blockage is further on, between the chamber and the septic tank or the network. If the chamber is empty but water stands in the house, the fault is between the house and the chamber or in the house itself. This one check helps to choose the right job and to write it in the request.

Grease, oil and coffee grounds settle on the pipe walls and form blockages, so they should be thrown in the bin, and a strainer for food scraps helps in the kitchen sink. A rotating snake breaks up grease deposits, cuts roots and clears the dirt, while jetting with a high-pressure water jet removes dirt from the pipe walls as well. When a snake is not enough, read about drain jetting.

Yard drain cleaning up to a chamber or septic tank

The yard line is cleaned from a chamber or from the inspection point of the house outlet. In an external sewer, chambers are placed at connection points where the direction, fall or diameter changes, and on straight sections every 35 m, if the pipe is smaller than 200 mm. If there are no chambers between the house and the septic tank, the snake has to go the full length of the line, and the work is slower.

If blockages repeat in the same place, the cause is often the line itself: a sagging section where water stands, roots in a joint or a pipe laid too shallow. Cleaning then helps for a while, but the solution is replacing the section, described on the page sewer pipe replacement. For a new house or a rebuilt line, read about connecting a house drain to a septic tank.

Sewer smell and drain ventilation

A smell in the house is often caused by a dried-out trap. For a sink trap the maker Geberit gives a water seal 50 mm deep, and UK guidance, for example, states that at least 25 mm of water must remain in the trap, otherwise sewer air gets into the room. In fixtures that are not used for a long time (a guest bathroom, a summer house, a floor drain) the water in the trap evaporates, and the smell appears by itself.

Another cause is ventilation. The drains are ventilated through a riser whose end is at least 0.5 m above a pitched roof, and it must not be joined to room ventilation or a flue. In buildings of up to five storeys, a sewer vacuum valve (an air admittance valve) can be put on the end of an unventilated riser. The condition is that the house has at least one ventilated riser and the valve is above the top edge of the highest fixture. More on the causes: sewer smell in the house.

Drain inspection hatch: why it is needed for cleaning

An inspection point is an opening through which a snake or a jetting hose is fed into the line. LBN 221-15 requires inspection points at the bottom and top of risers, in riser sections in the basement, at the start of branches if three or more fixtures are connected, and at bends. On horizontal sections of pipe up to 100 mm the distance between inspection points is up to 12 m.

If an inspection point is tiled over or missing, the cleaning has to be done through the toilet or a trap, and that takes longer. Chambers in domestic sewerage inside a building are forbidden, so in a basement an inspection hatch is suitable, not a chamber. Building an inspection point can be requested together with the cleaning.

What to write in the request

  • where the water stands: one fixture, several or the lowest place in the house;
  • a house or a flat, the number of floors, whether there is a basement;
  • where the wastewater goes: a septic tank, a holding tank, a biological treatment unit or the central sewer;
  • whether there is a chamber in the yard and whether it is full;
  • the water source (the central water supply or your own well or borehole), because with a hydrophore (a pump with a pressure tank) the water can be switched off at the pump;
  • what has already been tried: the trap, chemicals, a plunger.

Photos cannot be added to the form, so describe it in words. While you wait, do not use fixtures whose water goes to the blocked line, especially the washing machine and dishwasher.

What often goes wrong

  • Only the trap is cleaned although the fault is in the horizontal line, and a week later the water stands again.
  • Rainwater from downpipes is connected to the domestic sewer. In Ādaži Municipality rainwater may be led into the sewer only with the operator's permission, and it is forbidden to pour it into chambers.
  • The lid of a yard chamber is buried or paved over, and when the line blocks, the chamber has to be found first.
  • A full septic tank is taken for a blocked line, and a pipe with nothing wrong in it is cleaned.

Binding regulations No. 72/2022 of Ādaži Municipality on the procedure for public water utility services (amended by No. 11/2026): rainwater may be led into the sewer only with the operator's permission. Checked 13.11.2026.

Frequently asked questions

How much does drain cleaning in a house cost?

A blockage in the internal drains of the house, a toilet or a kitchen sink costs from €50 per call-out. A yard line to a chamber or septic tank costs from €50 for the first hour, jetting from €60 an hour. Materials and travel outside Ādaži are extra.

Do you also clean the line in the yard between the house and the septic tank?

Yes, it is cleaned with a motorised snake from a chamber or an inspection point. Emptying and installing a septic tank is a different job, done by tanker operators and septic tank installers.

Why does the toilet gurgle when the bath drains?

Water that cannot drain away along the line pushes air out through another trap. It is usually a sign of a partly blocked horizontal line or missing ventilation. If other fixtures also drain slowly at the same time, write that in the request.

Can drains be cleaned without an inspection hatch?

They can, through the toilet or a trap, but it takes more time, and a long line cannot always be cleaned to the end. If there is no inspection point, it can be built together with the cleaning.
