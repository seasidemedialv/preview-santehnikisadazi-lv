Radiator not heating - what to check

A radiator is usually not heating because of air, sludge, a closed valve or too low system pressure. Feel the radiator: a cold top usually means air, a cold bottom with a warm top means sludge. Then look at the boiler pressure gauge to see whether the system has pressure. If all radiators are cold, the fault is usually in the boiler, the pump or the pressure, not in the radiator.

How to find the cause from which part is cold

What you feel Possible cause What to do
Top cold, bottom warm air in the radiator bleed it
Top warm, bottom cold sludge (deposits) flush the system, not bleed it
The whole radiator is cold, the others are warm a closed or seized valve or thermostatic head check that the head is open and the valve moves
All radiators weak, the gauge shows little system pressure too low top up the system to the pressure the maker states
Colder upstairs than downstairs air at the top of the system, pressure too low bleed at the top, check the pressure
The pump is noisy, radiators gurgle air in the system bleed the radiators, check the pressure

How to bleed a radiator

  1. Switch off the heating. Let the radiators cool.
  2. Open the bleed valve. With a key, turn the valve in the top corner of the radiator, holding a cloth or a container under it.
  3. Close it when water runs. When the hissing stops and water starts to flow steadily, close the valve.
  4. Check the pressure. After bleeding, the system pressure usually drops. Look at the boiler pressure gauge and, if needed, top up the system.
  5. Switch the heating on. After a while, feel the radiator again, at the top and at the bottom.

The bleeding procedure and the pressure check follow the maker Worcester Bosch's instructions. Checked 13.11.2026.

What the boiler pressure gauge shows

In a closed heating system, cold, the pressure should usually be 1–2 bar: Vaillant states 1.0–2.0 bar, Worcester Bosch advises filling to 1–1.5 bar. If the system spans several floors, a higher pressure is needed: Vaillant states that every 10 m of height above the boiler adds about 1 bar.

The warning limits depend on the model. For example, the Vaillant ecoTEC exclusive shows a low-pressure warning below 0.8 bar and switches off below 0.5 bar. The safety valve of a wall-hung heating boiler on the heating circuit usually operates at around 3 bar (Worcester Bosch); for the Vaillant ecoTEC exclusive the permitted total pressure is 2.5 bar.

If the pressure keeps dropping, there is a leak in the system or the expansion vessel is not working. Flamco advises checking the vessel and its pre-charge every year, when the system has cooled and is without pressure. The system is filled so that the pressure is 0.2 bar higher than the vessel pre-charge. If water comes out of the vessel valve, Vaillant states that the vessel has to be replaced.

Sludge and flushing

If a radiator is warm at the top but cold at the bottom, Worcester Bosch advises cleaning the system, not bleeding. Vaillant advises testing the heating water with a magnetic rod: if it picks up magnetite (iron oxide), the system should be flushed and protected against corrosion with an inhibitor, or a magnetic filter should be fitted.

The inhibitor is added after flushing according to its maker's instructions. Fernox states that under the British standard BS 7593:2019 the heating water should be tested every year. About how the work goes: heating system flushing.

Circulation pump and air

For the ALPHA2 circulation pump the maker Grundfos states that it bleeds itself. Air in the pump can cause noise that goes away after a few minutes of running; for faster bleeding, speed III is set briefly. The pump must not run dry, and the system cannot be bled through the pump.

What not to do

  • Do not bleed a hot radiator in a running system: first switch the heating off and let it cool.
  • Do not fill the system above the pressure the maker states, otherwise the safety valve may operate.
  • Do not run the circulation pump dry, and do not try to bleed the system through it.
  • Do not measure the expansion vessel pre-charge with the system under pressure: it is measured with the system cooled and without pressure.
  • Do not bleed again and again without looking for the cause: if air keeps coming back, something in the system is not right.

When to call a plumber and what it costs

You need a plumber if the radiator is still cold after bleeding, if the pressure keeps dropping, if the radiator is cold at the bottom, or if the valve does not move. The price of bleeding radiators depends on the number of radiators, on whether the system pressure has to be topped up, and on whether the air keeps returning and the cause has to be found. The price of flushing a heating system depends on the system volume and the number of radiators or circuits, on whether the flushing is chemical or hydraulic, on cleaning the boiler heat exchanger, and on the price of the inhibitor and the new heat-transfer fluid.

Installing or replacing a radiator costs from €75, and a plumber call-out from €40. This is the labour price; materials are extra.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the pressure drop after bleeding?

Bleeding lets air and a little water out of the system, so the pressure usually drops. Look at the boiler pressure gauge and, if needed, top up the system to the pressure the maker states.

How often should the expansion vessel be checked?

Flamco advises every year. The pre-charge is measured when the system has cooled and is without pressure.

Will a magnetic filter help?

If there is magnetite in the heating water, Vaillant advises flushing the system and protecting it against corrosion, or fitting a magnetic filter.

Why are all the radiators cold?

Then the fault is usually not in the radiators. Check whether the boiler is running, whether the gauge shows too low a pressure, and whether the circulation pump is working.
