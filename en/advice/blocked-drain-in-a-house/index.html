Blocked drain in a house - what to check before a call-out

A blocked drain in a house first has to be located: is the water not draining at one fixture or in the whole house. If one trap is blocked, you can try to clear it yourself. If water rises in the lowest fixture, the fault is in the horizontal drain, the house outlet, the yard sewer line or the septic tank, and the yard inspection chamber helps to tell which. Until it is clear, do not use the washing machine or dishwasher.

What to do yourself before a call-out

  1. Stop using water. Switch off the washing machine and dishwasher and warn the others in the house. Every litre of water let out ends up in the same blocked line.
  2. Find the lowest fixture. In the basement, sauna or ground floor, water starts to rise first. This helps you see whether the blockage is in the shared line.
  3. Clear the trap. Put a bucket under the sink and unscrew the trap: it should be removable or have a cleaning opening. In a shower or bath trap, Geberit advises pulling hair out with long tweezers.
  4. Open the yard inspection chamber. Look at whether water stands or flows in it. A full chamber means a blockage further along, an empty one means a blockage between the house and the chamber.
  5. Check the septic tank. Remember when it was last pumped out. In Ādaži Municipality (binding regulations No. 3/2025) and Ropaži Municipality (binding regulations No. 45/22) a septic tank and a holding tank must be emptied at least once a year; only a registered sewage collector may carry it away.

Why drains in a house get blocked

Fat, oil and coffee grounds settle on the pipe walls and form blockages. SIA “Rīgas ūdens” advises throwing them in the rubbish and using a strainer for food waste in the kitchen sink. Wet wipes, hygiene products, cotton wool, food and hair get into the line and hold back the rest; for Sololift2 pumps Grundfos states that these things damage the unit.

Drains must be built with an even slope: LBN 221-15 gives, without calculation, 30 ‰ (3 cm per metre) for pipes up to 50 mm and 20 ‰ (2 cm per metre) for 75–100 mm pipes, and the slope must not change between connections. In a yard line, tree roots can also cause a blockage.

In winter a line can freeze. LBN 223-15 requires an external gravity pipe to be laid no shallower than 0.7 m to the pipe surface, and shallower pipes have to be protected from frost. If a line has frozen, see frozen pipes.

LBN 221-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 332), clauses 232, 269 and 272; LBN 223-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 327), clause 67. Checked 13.11.2026.

Inspection points and chambers: where to reach the line

So that a line can be cleaned, LBN 221-15 requires inspection or cleaning access plugs at the bottom and top of risers, on riser sections in the basement, at the start of branches if three or more fixtures are connected, and at bends. On horizontal sections with pipes up to 100 mm, the distance between inspection points may be up to 12 m.

In the yard, inspection chambers are placed at connection points and where the direction, slope or diameter changes, and on straight sections every 35 m if the pipe is smaller than 200 mm. If a chamber is buried or cannot be found, cleaning becomes more difficult, so note that in the form.

LBN 221-15, clauses 253 and 254; LBN 223-15, clause 73. Checked 13.11.2026.

How a blockage is cleared

A blockage is cleared with a drain snake; if that does not help, the line is flushed with a high-pressure machine. The US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) describes that a rotating coil breaks up fat deposits, cuts roots and frees debris, while high-pressure jetting with a jet of water removes dirt and fat from the pipe walls and is effective for small-diameter pipes. More on jetting: drain jetting.

What not to do

  • Do not flush the toilet again if the water is already rising: it can overflow.
  • Do not open an inspection point in a low place while the line is full: sewage will flow out into the room.
  • Do not pour fat, oil or coffee grounds down the drain.
  • Do not put wet wipes and hygiene products down the toilet.
  • Do not take away the septic tank contents yourself: only a sewage collector registered with the municipality may do that.

When to call a plumber and what it costs

You need a plumber if clearing the trap does not help, if water rises in several fixtures, or if the yard inspection chamber is empty but water stands in the house. Clearing a blockage in the internal drains costs from €50, clearing a toilet blockage from €50, cleaning an outdoor (yard) sewer line from €50 for the first working hour, high-pressure jetting from €60 per hour (min. 1 h). This is the labour price; materials are extra.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell whether the septic tank is to blame?

If the yard inspection chamber is full and the water in the septic tank stands high, the blockage is in the septic tank or the line up to it, or the septic tank is full. Then it first needs pumping out, which only a registered sewage collector may do.

How much does cleaning a yard sewer line cost?

Cleaning the outdoor sewer line between the house and the inspection chamber or septic tank costs from €50 for the first working hour with a motorised drain snake. High-pressure jetting costs from €60 per hour.

When is jetting needed instead of a drain snake?

A snake breaks through the blockage and cuts roots. If the line blocks again and again or the walls are coated with fat, high-pressure jetting removes that.

Can a sewer line freeze?

It can, if the pipe lies shallow or runs through an unheated room. LBN 223-15 requires the external pipe to be laid no shallower than 0.7 m to the surface, and shallower pipes to be protected from frost.
